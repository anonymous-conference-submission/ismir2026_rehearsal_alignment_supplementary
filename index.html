Practice Makes Imperfect: Towards Robust
Symbolic Rehearsal-to-Score Alignment

Supplementary Materials — ISMIR 2026

Abstract

Symbolic performance-to-score alignment is a core research area in Music Information Retrieval (MIR), supporting applications such as expressive performance analysis and interactive accompaniment systems. Existing methods, however, are largely designed for complete, linear performances that closely follow the score, and their flexibility in rehearsal scenarios remains underexplored. Piano rehearsals are inherently non-linear, involving repetitions, errors, and arbitrary jumps, which contradict the assumptions of conventional alignment approaches, thereby posing challenges to robust alignment. In this paper, we introduce the task of symbolic rehearsal-to-score alignment and investigate the alignment of the Rach3 MIDI Dataset piano rehearsals with their corresponding scores. We analyze the limitations of existing alignment methods in this context and propose and evaluate methodological adaptations of Hidden Markov Models to better handle fragmentary and error-prone practice behavior. Additionally, we release the alignments for the piano rehearsals in the Rach3 MIDI Dataset. By doing so, this work aims to establish a foundation upon which rehearsal-aware MIR systems and data-driven studies of piano rehearsal can be developed.
